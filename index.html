History of the Institution

St. Hubert Seminary Senior High School was established in October 1970 by the late Rt. Rev. Peter Akwasi Sarpong, then Bishop of the Catholic Diocese of Kumasi. The institution was originally conceived as a minor seminary to nurture young men with an interest in the Catholic priesthood, combining rigorous academic training with deep spiritual formation.

For many years, the school operated as a private mission institution, gaining a reputation across the Ashanti Region for its strict discipline and high moral standards. In 2005, a significant milestone was reached when the school was formally absorbed into the public education system by the Ghana Education Service (GES). This transition allowed the school to expand its facilities and offer a broader range of academic programs, including General Science, General Arts, and Business.

Today, St. Hubert Seminary SHS stands as one of the premier Category 'A' schools in Ghana. It consistently produces outstanding results in the West African Senior School Certificate Examination (WASSCE) and excels in extracurricular activities like debating and sports. While it now welcomes a diverse student body, it proudly maintains its Catholic heritage, continuing to produce graduates who are not only intellectually capable but also morally upright leaders serving in various sectors of Ghanaian society.
